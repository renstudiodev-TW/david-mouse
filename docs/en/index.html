Watch YouTube on a head-mouse,
without accidental pauses.

David Mouse is a small Windows tool made for people who control their cursor with head movement (head-mouse / eye-gaze). It solves the most common frustration: tiny head jitter triggering accidental auto-clicks — your music keeps pausing, your videos keep stopping. Hit one big button to pause auto-click, and resume it when you need to interact again.

💛 Why this exists — David's Story

"If even the most basic pleasures are taken away, where does the weight of life rest?"

This software was built because of David

In my career as an assistive technology evaluator, David Chang's story — a person with cervical spinal cord injury (SCI C-level) — is one that has left the deepest impression on me.

When I first met David, he was in bad shape. The spinal cord injury from a car accident had stripped him of control over his body, leaving him entirely dependent on his wife around the clock. In that small room, what was trapped wasn't only David's immobilized body — it was also the unbearable pressure on his wife. As an occupational therapist, I knew my job wasn't just to help him "maintain vital signs," but to help him reclaim the feeling of having a life.

To help him reconnect with the world, we tried to secure a head-mouse for him through charitable funding. But we ran into a wall. Wheelchairs and electric beds — "traditional" assistive devices — were gladly supported. The moment we mentioned a "mouse," though, the room cooled: that was entertainment, not a basic necessity.

That gap in social perception struck me deeply. Many people think a person with a severe disability just needs to survive and be able to move. They overlook the fact that enjoyment and autonomy are often what keep a person going. For David, browsing the web, watching videos, and chatting with the outside world by typing — these weren't luxuries. They were the only tiny window through which he could reclaim any sense of control over his own life.

Fortunately, we didn't give up. We eventually found other resources and managed to get David his head-mouse hardware. But once the hardware was in place, the real challenge emerged on the software side.

We started with point-n-click software. It's powerful and full-featured, but for a non-technical family caregiver, the advanced settings were simply too complex. David's wife was already exhausted from daily caregiving — she couldn't reasonably be expected to fine-tune layer upon layer of configuration. Worse, even after painstakingly getting everything set up, the software still produced uncontrolled auto-clicks. Imagine: David finally gets a video to start playing, full of expectation — and the screen keeps cycling through play, pause, play, pause in an endless loop.

Watching the hope in his eyes turn to helpless frustration, the thought surfaced: "Why don't we just build our own?"

We decided to build a tool centered on subtraction design — keeping only the most essential features — and named it "david-mouse" after him. The goal was clear: the user and their family should be able to set it up intuitively, without always waiting for a professional to come help. We even added multilingual support, because our quiet hope was that not just David, but other people with spinal cord injuries anywhere in the world could download it for free and reclaim their digital autonomy.

After "david-mouse" was finished, David's life genuinely changed. He can now navigate the web and find entertainment on his own, using just subtle head movements. Not only did this give his wife room to breathe — more importantly, the light in David's eyes came back. The irritable, depressed David slowly faded, because that small mouse had given him back the feeling of being in control. He began pushing himself to speak louder and more clearly. He started wanting to sing. He wanted to control more things through his own effort.

Riding that wave of motivation, we later helped modify his electric wheelchair's controller. Today, David can confidently take himself out in his wheelchair on his own. From a tiny cursor on a screen to a wheelchair moving freely in the real world — "david-mouse" may be just a simple piece of software, but for David, it was the key to restarting his life. And it's the gift we want to give to people with spinal cord injuries everywhere.

🎯 Core Features

Everything is designed around one principle: a head-mouse user should be able to operate it without strain. All buttons are large, high-contrast, and trigger via dwell (cursor stays still).

⏯️

One-click pause

A huge PAUSE button. Dwell once to pause before YouTube, dwell again on the same button to resume. The whole flow works without ever needing a physical mouse.

🎚️

Adjustable dwell time

Slide between 0.5 and 3 seconds. Everyone's head stability is different — find what works for you.

🖱️

Four action shortcuts

Left click, right click, double click, pause/resume — each with a 2.5-second countdown so you have time to move the cursor to the target.

📐

Snap to any corner

The arrows in the window corners jump the app to the top-left, top-right, bottom-left, or bottom-right of your screen — out of the way of whatever you're doing.

🌐

Four-language UI

Switch between 繁體中文 / English / 日本語 / 한국어 right inside the app. Works for users and family members across languages.

🚀

Start with Windows

Tick the box once and it launches automatically every time you boot. No more manual startup.

📖 How to use it

Three steps. If you're about to watch YouTube, remember to **press PAUSE first** to stop auto-click.

1

Set your dwell time

Use the slider to choose how long the cursor must stay still before triggering a click. Try 1.0s to start. If your head is very stable, go shorter (0.5s). If less stable, go longer (2s+).

2

Press PAUSE before watching YouTube

Move your cursor onto the green "● AUTO-CLICK ON" button and let it dwell ~1 second — it auto-clicks itself and the button turns red "⏸ PAUSED". Now you can watch your video in peace without random pauses.

💡 To resume later: just dwell on the same red button again. While paused, that button is the only thing on screen that responds to dwell — so even if your head moves around the screen while watching, nothing else can be clicked by accident.

3

Need to click something? Use an action button

Dwell on Left / Right / Double for ~1 second → button turns yellow with a countdown → during the 2.5 seconds, move your cursor to wherever you want the click to land → it fires automatically at the end.

⬇ Install & Run

For now, you run from source with Python. A single-file .exe installer will be released soon.

🏃 Quick start (recommended)

No need to pre-install Python — install.bat will auto-install it for you (per-user, no admin needed) if it isn't already there:

# 1. Get the source
git clone https://github.com/renstudiodev-TW/david-mouse
cd david-mouse

# 2. One-click install (handles Python + deps)
install.bat

# 3. Run it
run.bat

🛠️ Build a standalone .exe

If you want a single .exe that doesn't need Python installed:

# 1. Install PyInstaller
pip install pyinstaller

# 2. Build
build.bat

# 3. Find the exe in
dist\david-mouse.exe
